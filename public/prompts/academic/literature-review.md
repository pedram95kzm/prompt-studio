## Evidence rules

- Ask at most three questions related to my topic if the question, purpose, or evidence boundaries need clarification. Avoid a generic interview and do not repeat information already provided.
- Separate planning a review from actually conducting one. If sources are absent or unavailable, deliver a search and synthesis plan; do not claim to have reviewed the literature.
- Identify the evidence available for each source: title/metadata, abstract, excerpt, or full text. A title or abstract does not support claims about unprovided details or the full study.
- If browsing or database tools are available, state where you searched, when, the queries used, and practical access limits. Do not claim exhaustive coverage unless the process supports it.
- Never invent papers, authors, publication details, DOIs, quotes, findings, sample sizes, or citations. Mark missing metadata and unverifiable claims explicitly.
- Distinguish empirical findings, theoretical arguments, author interpretations, and your own synthesis. Compare study quality and limitations in ways appropriate to the discipline.
- Do not label a narrative or exploratory overview a systematic review. Formal systematic-review claims require a recorded, reproducible search, screening, and appraisal process.

## Deliverable

1. A narrowed question, intended review type, scope, and explicit assumptions.
2. Relevant search concepts, synonyms, example queries, candidate databases or repositories, and inclusion/exclusion criteria.
3. A screening and extraction table: source identifier | available evidence | question | method/material | finding or argument | limitations | relevance. Populate it only from accessible sources; otherwise provide an empty reusable structure.
4. If evidence is available, a thematic synthesis showing agreements, disagreements, methodological differences, and unresolved questions, with source identifiers attached to claims. Otherwise, provide a proposed outline clearly labeled as a plan.
5. Potential gaps labeled UNVERIFIED unless supported by the actual reviewed evidence, plus the searches needed to investigate them.
6. A short next-step checklist and an honest account of what the available evidence cannot establish.

Use the requested citation style only for verified metadata. Keep recommendations separate from findings and avoid reproducing lengthy source passages.
